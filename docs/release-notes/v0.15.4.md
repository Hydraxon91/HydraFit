# 0.15.4

## New

- **Your set counts now shape the week.** Weekly volume is now counted per muscle, sized to the muscle, and
  spread across the days that train it. Choosing more sets per exercise gives you fewer, longer exercises
  instead of the same six exercises with more sets each, and fewer sets gives you more exercises. Biceps and
  triceps still get their weekly direct sets at every set count.
- **Leg extension and leg curl on every lower or legs day**, when those machines are in your equipment.
  Squats and hinges do not replace them.
- **Shrugs in your plan.** Pull and upper days get one shrug as an accessory. Neck exercises are still
  logged by hand and are not planned.
- **Three exercises.** Wide-Grip Bench Press, Bench Dips and Feet-Elevated Bench Dips, each with setup and
  execution instructions. The two dips count as direct triceps work, and you can add weight to them.
- **Your plan settings are remembered.** Sets per exercise, accessory sets per exercise and automatic
  per-set targets now stay as you set them after you close the app.

## Improved

- **Planner.** Generated plans no longer pick duplicate or novelty versions of standard lifts, such as Push-Up
  Wide, Scissor Kick, Bicycle Crunch, Standing Military Press or Cable Chest Press. Those exercises are still
  in the Library and you can still log or add them yourself; they are just never planned automatically.
  Custom exercises you create are planned as before. Exercises you have excluded stay excluded.
- **Weekly volume per muscle.** Targets now follow published training-volume guidance: on Balanced about 12
  weekly sets for chest, back and quads, 10 for hamstrings and side delts, 8 for glutes, arms, calves and
  rear delts, and less for muscles that presses, rows and hinges already train.
- **Workout length.** Compound lifts and ordinary accessories share up to 6 places a day. The planned
  essentials (direct arm work, the shrug, the leg extension and leg curl) come on top, up to 8 in all.
- **Chest on upper/lower weeks.** Upper days can now include a chest fly when chest is short of its weekly
  sets, since that split has no push day.
- **Supersets.** Pairing now covers all the planned curls, extensions and dips, and moves a biceps and a
  triceps exercise next to each other when a day has both, so pairs actually form. Push and pull days
  train one arm each, so they still have no pair.
- **Biceps and triceps sets** follow your accessory sets, raised only when your split has too few arm slots
  to reach the weekly arm target. On a 4-day upper/lower week at 2 accessory sets they are still 3.
- **Groq planner.** It now gets the same weekly targets and day limits, sees which exercises you prefer or
  want less of, and its plans get the same set counts as the built-in planner, including one fewer set for
  sore muscles.
- **Split builder.** Days per week, sets per exercise and accessory sets per exercise now sit above the
  generated week, with **Regenerate** next to them, and always show the counts the planner actually uses.
  Automatic per-set targets, superset pairing and the planning dates are under **Show configuration**.
- **Workout card.** The separate **Watch demo** buttons on Train are gone, since the exercise details already
  offer **Watch a demo on YouTube**. This also stops the guided card from squeezing its buttons.

## Good to know

- A plan you already accepted is not changed. Tap **Regenerate** in the Split builder to get one with the
  new rules.
- Your saved sets and automatic per-set targets are not part of backups; restoring a backup leaves them as
  they are.

## Known limitations

- The new planner rules are checked with automated tests across every goal, 2 to 6 days and 1 to 6 sets, but
  not yet widely on a phone. If a week looks thin for a muscle, tell us which one.
- At 4 or more sets per exercise, an upper day can reach 8 exercises and over 30 working sets. Lower the
  sets per exercise if a day is too long.
- The plank and hanging leg raise can appear on every lower day, because they are the only planned
  exercises for obliques and hip flexors.
- The new exercises have no hand-picked demo video yet, so their demo link opens a YouTube search.
